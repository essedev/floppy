01 · Overview

One disk, shared by the whole team

Floppy shows up in the Finder as a disk. The files live as blocks on S3 object storage; the file tree lives in a central Valkey, with a live copy inside a small Linux VM on every Mac; each person signs in, and a menu bar app installs and drives everything. This page describes that target. Why this design and not another, and how it came about, are in ARCHITECTURE.md, DECISIONS.md and CYCLES.md.

02 · How it works

How it works, in plain words

Floppy looks like a disk, but nothing is copied to the Mac until someone needs it. Two things travel apart: the list of files and folders, the file tree, which is small and kept on every Mac; and the contents of the files, which stay on S3 and arrive in pieces.

Opening a folder

To show a folder the Finder asks thousands of small questions: names, sizes, icons. The copy of the file tree inside the Mac answers them, so a folder of 300 files appears in a fraction of a second, even one a colleague has just filled.

Playing a clip

Only the part being watched is downloaded, in blocks of 1 MB, a little ahead of the playhead. What arrives stays on the Mac's disk: the second time, the clip plays as from a local disk.

Scrubbing the timeline

A jump lands on a block that may not be on the Mac yet. Floppy fetches that one block first, then the next few: about 0.4 s on a 100 Mbps line, without waiting for the rest of the file.

Copying footage in

The file is cut into blocks and uploaded; it counts as saved only once all of it is on S3. The blocks also stay on the Mac that copied them, so whoever loads the footage can start editing at once.

A colleague sees it

Every change goes to the central server, which passes it on to every Mac's copy of the file tree as it happens. The new file shows up on the other Macs within a hundredth of a second; its contents download when someone opens it.

The line drops

The copy of the file tree stays: folders can still be browsed and clips already on the Mac still play. Saving fails at once with a clear message instead of hanging, and the disk comes back on its own within a second of the line returning.

Blocks, and why they keep video smooth

  • A file is cut into blocks of 1 MB on S3. A phone clip of 434 MB at 60 Mbps is 434 blocks, and playing it uses about 7 or 8 of them a second.
  • Playing downloads only the blocks it needs, plus 4 MB ahead, and keeps them in the cache on the Mac's disk.
  • A jump in the timeline costs one small block, not the whole file: on a 100 Mbps line the first picture after a jump comes in about 0.4 s.
  • Make available downloads every block of a project ahead of time, to edit with no wait, or without a network.
  • The cache has a ceiling worked out from the Mac's free space. When it is full, the blocks used least recently make room first; Free up space empties it for one project. Nothing leaves S3.

03 · Architecture

Three places, and the file tree on both ends

Each Mac runs the app, the floppy command and a small Linux VM with JuiceFS. The file tree lives in a central Valkey, and the VM keeps a replica of it that the master updates as it changes: JuiceFS reads the tree from that replica, sends its writes to the center, and fetches file contents from S3. The control plane only talks to the app and, as administrator, to Valkey.

05 · The JuiceFS patches

Two patches on JuiceFS, and why

JuiceFS Community does the file system: data as blocks on S3, metadata in a Redis-compatible server, a cache on each client. Floppy runs it at v1.4.1 with two patches of its own, kept in spike/juicefs-replica/ and applied in order when the binary is built. They change where JuiceFS reads its metadata, how it commits a write and how it groups a reader's pieces into block downloads; the volume format, the layout on S3 and the metadata keys stay JuiceFS's own, plus one counter per session. Without the replica option a patched JuiceFS behaves like upstream.

Why upstream JuiceFS does not fit

  • Samba asks a lot. To list 300 files for the Finder it asks JuiceFS about 2,700 questions: ._ names that do not exist, extended attributes. With the metadata across a 15 ms link each one is a round trip, about 75 s for one listing.
  • No local replica for a read-write mount. Upstream reads from Redis replicas only on read-only mounts, through Sentinel or Cluster and route-read=replica, and has no way to read from a replica and still see its own writes.
  • A write is several round trips. WATCH, the reads, MULTI/EXEC and UNWATCH: 3 to 6 trips across the link for every create, rename or save.
  • The Mac reads out of order. Through its cache the macOS SMB client sends 128 KB pieces out of order, up to 40 MB ahead. JuiceFS turned each piece into a range load, which it does not cache, and downloaded the same block again moments later.
  • The client-side cache of JuiceFS 1.4 is not enough. It caches attributes and lookups of existing names, not listings, extended attributes or missing names, and leaves writes as they are (DECISIONS #5).

0001: metadata from a local replica

pkg/meta · about 1,100 lines of Go, 970 of tests

A Valkey replica of the central master runs inside each Mac's VM. The patch lets a read-write mount read from it, keeps every read consistent with the Mac's own writes, and commits each write in one round trip. It is switched on in the metadata URL:

redis://master:6379/1?read-replica=redis://127.0.0.1:6380&max-lag=2s&master-timeout=10s

Reads, and seeing your own writes
  • The replica serves the reads made outside a transaction: lookups, attributes, directory listings, extended attributes, the slice lists of a file, the counters behind statfs. Locks, sessions, quotas and directory stats, gc, fsck, dump and load stay on the master.
  • Read-your-writes with no extra round trip: a hook adds HINCRBY ryw<sid> <field> as the first command of every MULTI/EXEC, and the client keeps the highest value returned.
  • A read goes to the replica only once the replica holds that value. Otherwise it waits up to one master round trip, then reads from the master.
  • The field is random for each client and each reset, so a replica holding an earlier life of the same session never counts as fresh.
Writes as optimistic transactions
  • The first attempt of each transaction runs JuiceFS's own Go function unchanged, with its reads served by the replica and recorded.
  • MULTI/EXEC becomes one EVALSHA of a generic Lua script: it compares every recorded read with the master's current value, and only then applies the writes and the read-your-writes counter.
  • A changed value is a conflict, and the transaction runs again the classic way, with WATCH on the master. So does anything the script cannot check or replay: SCAN and HSCAN, a write outside MULTI/EXEC, a command over 4,000 arguments.
  • One generic script rather than Lua per operation: permissions, ACLs, trash and quotas keep a single implementation, in Go.

0002: whole blocks for out-of-order readers

pkg/vfs/reader.go · 14 lines changed, a 119-line test

When a read falls inside a sequential session, the patch widens the slice JuiceFS creates for it to the whole block that holds it: one slice per block. The small pieces the Mac sends out of order then join that slice instead of each loading a range of its own. Range loads are not cached, so the stream used to download the same block again moments later, and the extra slices pushed JuiceFS's request cleanup into cancelling downloads halfway. Random reads are untouched. On a clip, the download beyond what it plays stays at 4-6%.

How they are tested

  • spike/juicefs-replica/test.sh runs the Go integration tests of 0001 against a real Valkey master and replica in Docker: read-your-writes, concurrent writers, a link that drops, lag, a master that goes down or answers slowly, writes while offline, round trips per operation, optimistic transactions and their conflicts, and JuiceFS's whole metadata test suite with reads on the replica, also with replication delayed by 5 ms.
  • The same script runs the pkg/vfs tests with 0002's own: small pieces sent out of order inside a sequential stream must load whole blocks.
  • Then a mount check: format, mount with a replica, create and list 200 files, rename one, remove them all, and the replica must have served reads.
  • spike/acceptance.py runs the 17 checks under Performance: two clients, the Mac over SMB, a shaped link.

Keeping them alive

  • JuiceFS stays pinned at v1.4.1 and moves only when a release brings something needed (a security or bug fix, performance), not with every Floppy release.
  • The patches move to a branch of a fork (essedev/juicefs), one commit per change, so an update is a rebase.
  • A scheduled GitHub Actions job applies them to each new JuiceFS release and runs the Go tests, so a break shows up before the update is needed.
  • An update ships only after the Go tests, JuiceFS's own Redis metadata tests, the acceptance suite and a run with mixed versions (some Macs on the old JuiceFS, some on the new, one Valkey); if mixed versions do not hold, the control plane enforces a minimum version.
  • Checked on 17/10/2026: against JuiceFS main, 25 of 0001's 27 hunks in redis.go apply; doCleanStaleSession and doRemoveXattr conflict.
  • Alongside, the read replica and the optimistic transactions are to be proposed upstream, so the fork does not have to live forever.

A revoked Mac keeps the replica it had, S3 key included, until the app clears it at the next heartbeat; whoever copied the key can delete or download raw blocks until the admin deletes it at the provider. Without metadata the blocks are 1 MB fragments with no names; deletion is the real risk. Mitigations: rotate right after revoking, and bucket versioning as a safety net (an open decision, it bills deleted blocks).

Permissions stay all or nothing

Everyone in an organization sees and can delete every file of its volumes. JuiceFS Community has no per-folder permissions; trash (7 days) and the bucket's copy at a second provider cover mistakes. The replicas in the Macs are not a backup: they follow the master, deletions included.

Slow is not unreachable

A master that answers late, for example while a large copy fills the uplink, does not turn the disk read only: writes only get slower. The disk goes read only when the master is unreachable beyond doubt, or after 10 s without a single byte from it (see The JuiceFS patches). Measured (t13): with the master answering in 1.2 s, give or take 0.6 s, 20 writes went through with no read-only error.

13 · Costs

Cost grows with terabytes, not people

Floppy has no per-person price. Self-hosted, a team pays the storage (about 7.5 EUR/TB on OVH) and a fixed 20-30 EUR for the metadata server, the central Valkey and its replica in the datacenter (an estimate: the host is not chosen yet). Adding a person costs nothing; adding a terabyte costs the storage. The commercial products charge per person.

What the licenses hide

The gap widens with every person, but what is left is the cost of running it: keeping the Valkey up and its backups checked, answering when a Mac does not mount, and carrying the JuiceFS patches across releases (half a day to a day per release that touches them, an estimate). For a small team that time is the real price; it pays off with more people, more terabytes, or data that must stay on infrastructure the team controls.

15 · Open decisions

Still to close before or during the build

Everything already decided, with the alternative it ruled out, is in DECISIONS.md.

Cache on revocation

The cache and the replica hold the organization's footage and file tree on a Mac that is no longer allowed.

ProposalThe app clears both at the first heartbeat that says revoked.
Bucket versioning

A safety net for the rotation window, but deleted and compacted blocks stay billed.

ProposalOn, with a lifecycle rule that expires old versions after a few days.
Where the central Valkey runs

A VPS next to the bucket in Milan, or a managed Valkey in the same region. It needs appendfsync always, a replica and replication open to the Macs.

ProposalDecided with the first instance, on durability first and price second.
The Unreachable text

When the center does not answer, the disk stays browsable and cached clips play: the panel must not read as if the disk were gone.

ProposalSay that the disk is read only until the line is back, and that saving will work again on its own.
